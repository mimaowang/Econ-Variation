# Field-top China regional and urban variation coverage

This is the live coverage map for the field-top collection requested by the project owner. It replaces the *general economics* five-journal campaign as the active target, while preserving `sources/top5-china-regional-urban-coverage.md` as a historical audit. “Field-top” is a practical scope choice, not a claim that there is one universally accepted ranking: the first three journals are direct regional/urban economics outlets, and the last two are leading economic-geography outlets where spatial organization, agglomeration, regional development, migration, and place-based institutions are central. The subsequent journal expansion is tracked separately in [`sources/china-regional-urban-journal-expansion.md`](china-regional-urban-journal-expansion.md), so widening the source universe does not blur the completion boundary or weaken the evidence gate for these five lanes.

The official journal pages establish each outlet’s scope and archive boundary. They do not establish that a particular paper contains a reusable variation; that decision still requires the paper, appendix, replication material, or an inspectable institutional source.

Publication year is a small ordering signal in this campaign: when otherwise comparable leads have similar China relevance, spatial substance, evidence access, and likely research value, a paper closer to 2026 may be screened first and its year should remain visible in the task/source record. Recency never repairs an implied assignment, and it never displaces an older paper that contributes a distinct or historically important variation.

The object is a paper-used institutional variation or assignment mechanism that can support a later research decision. A source is in scope when it changes exposure for Chinese units, directly exposes Chinese units through a global event, or documents a genuinely portable method for a Chinese regional/urban application. The paper must be triaged into exactly one role: `china-variation`, `global-china-variation`, `transferable-method`, or `skip`.

Do not treat a China sample, a city name, a policy mention, a title, an abstract, a theoretical counterfactual, or a plausible future application as a variation. For a retained source, screen first and keep uncertainty in the candidate ledger. Resolve identity before extraction: one canonical case follows one instrument, one implementation regime, and one primary assignment mechanism. A new active canonical record must meet the admission gate; an unresolved or paper-only lead remains a candidate, blocked, or skipped. Existing records are reconciliation and de-duplication targets, not evidence that this new lane is already complete.

The field scope is deliberately substantive rather than keyword-only. Search terms are recall aids and should be expanded with Chinese cities, provinces, prefectures, counties, transport corridors, hukou, land, housing, migration, agglomeration, industrial location, local public finance, environmental regulation, disasters, and regional inequality. A hit enters this campaign only when the geographic or urban mechanism matters to treatment, comparison, exposure, or the data connection.

## Relationship to `Econ Data Know-How`

The two repositories are complementary and must remain separate. Econ-Variation records what institution or event changed, who was assigned exposure, when and where it operated, how treatment and comparison can be encoded, what design it may support, and where the evidence or identification can fail. `Econ Data Know-How` records the data assets papers actually use, how to obtain or reconstruct them, coverage, identifiers and joins, access conditions, reproducibility, and data limitations. A DOI may point from one repository to the other, but do not copy canonical variation records into the data catalog, copy data-asset records here, or turn a data search into a second policy directory.

## Bounded collection loop

For each journal, work through one archive/index inventory at a time, then run the independent metadata or subject/citation lane and a repeat search. Reconcile DOI and title duplicates against `variations/` before opening a new screen. Discovery tasks may only create a source list and one screen task per retained source. Screen tasks may only end in `candidate`, `skipped`, or `blocked`; they do not write canonical files. Resolve/ground tasks close the identity, institutional boundary, assignment, timing, treatment, comparison, join keys, estimand, evidence limits, and principal failure conditions before admission.

The journal search should cover all years available in the named archive as of the recorded snapshot date. The map must name the endpoints searched, query date, publication-year coverage, recall terms or subject filters, and the disposition of every retained lead. If an archive or full text is inaccessible, record the exact blocker instead of filling the gap from a search snippet.

1. a primary archive/metadata sweep and an independent recall sweep;
