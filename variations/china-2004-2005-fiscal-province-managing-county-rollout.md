  china_relevance: >
    The reform is a Chinese sub-provincial fiscal-hierarchy change: provincial
    governments replaced the province-city-county ("province manages city, city
    manages county") fiscal arrangement with a direct province-county fiscal
    linkage, bypassing prefecture governments in revenue sharing, transfers,
    budgets, fund scheduling, and year-end settlement. The 2004-2005 first wave
    created staggered county exposure that regional and public-finance papers use
    to study fiscal incentives, monitoring, county expenditure structure, and
    county economic growth. The canonical boundary is the province-initiated
    regime before the 2009 central mandate; it excludes the parallel
    administrative empowerment ("qiangxian kuquan") reforms and the long-standing
    Zhejiang/Hainan arrangements.
identity:
  instrument: >
    The fiscal "province managing county" (fiscal PMC, 省直管县财政体制) reform
    removes the prefecture layer from the fiscal chain. Under the reform the
    province deals directly with each reformed county on revenue and expenditure
    division, transfer payments, budget and final-account reporting, treasury fund
    scheduling, year-end settlement, special-purpose grants, and debt repayment
    commitments. The county's fiscal accounts and reports go straight to the
    provincial finance department instead of passing through the prefecture, and
    the prefecture stops sharing or top-slicing the reformed county's revenues.
  authority: >
    Provincial party committees and provincial governments initiated and chose the
    scope of the 2004-2005 reforms within the existing administrative framework;
    the Ministry of Finance only later endorsed and generalized the model. The
    central 2009 opinion (Cai Yu [2009] No. 78) then converted the regime into a
    centrally directed national rollout targeted for completion by end-2012
    outside ethnic autonomous regions, which is a different implementation regime
    outside this record's boundary.

  implementation_regime: >
    Provinces adopted the reform at different times and in two modes. Anhui
    (decision 2004-01-21) and Hubei implemented it province-wide in one step from
    2004, with enumerated exceptions such as city districts, the counties of four
    Anhui mining/industrial cities, and Hubei's Enshi autonomous prefecture.
    Henan (2004), Hebei and Jiangxi (2005), and later Shanxi, Shaanxi, and Jilin
    piloted the reform in selected counties and expanded in phases; Jiangsu
    adopted it province-wide from 2007. All of this remained a province-initiated
    regime until the Ministry of Finance's June 2009 opinion set a national
    end-2012 target. Zhejiang and Hainan had long operated province-county fiscal
    arrangements and are generally treated as always-treated, not as part of this
    wave.
  assignment_mechanism: >
    A provincial government decision assigns counties to the reform, so adoption
    is staggered by province and, within phased provinces, selective by county.
    Provincial documents show selection was not random: Henan's 2004 pilot favored
    economically strong counties, Jiangxi and Shanxi piloted in national
    poverty-designated counties, and Shaanxi combined ecologically or fiscally
    distressed counties with two better-off counties. County exposure begins in
    the budget year stated in the provincial document. Because selection responds
    to county economic and fiscal characteristics, the assignment is a
    government-chosen rollout, not an eligibility threshold or random draw, and
    any causal design must address that selection explicitly.

assignment:
  unit: >
    County-level unit (county or county-level city; city districts are excluded
    because their fiscal autonomy and prefecture support differ), observed
    annually. Outcomes sit at the county-year level; the assignment decision sits
    with the province.
  treated: >
    Counties brought under the direct province-county fiscal linkage. In the
    2004-2005 first wave this means all eligible counties of Anhui and Hubei from
    2004, Henan's 2004 pilot counties, and Hebei's and Jiangxi's 2005 pilot
    counties. The JRS application by Jia, Ning, and Zhang (2023) reports treatment
    as counties in the provinces that reformed in 2004/2005; whether its coding is
    strictly province-level or county-by-county within phased provinces is not
    visible in the inspected abstract and remains unverified.
  comparison_pool: >
    Counties in provinces that had not adopted the reform, under county and year
    fixed effects. Counties in always-treated jurisdictions (Zhejiang, Hainan,
    the four direct-administered municipalities, and special cases such as Jiyuan
    in Henan, Tianmen/Qianjiang/Xiantao in Hubei, and Shihezi in Xinjiang) are
    excluded by the author team's verified coding. Non-reformed counties inside
    phased provinces are a contaminated comparison because the reform changes the
    prefecture's incentives toward all of its counties; Ning and Zhao (2019)
    exclude them from the control group for this reason.
  rule: >
    Treatment follows the provincial implementation documents: a county is
    treated from the fiscal year in which its revenue division, transfers, budget
    reporting, fund scheduling, and settlement switch from the prefecture channel
    to the direct provincial channel. The verified author-team convention codes
    reform status county-by-county from the provincial documents; the JRS paper's
    own coding rule awaits full-text verification.
  intensity: >
    Binary adoption in the published applications. A feasible intensity margin
    exists (county-level revenue-share changes and transfer bypass shares differ
    across provinces), but no inspected source constructs it.
  exemptions:
  - Zhejiang and Hainan, which long operated province-county fiscal arrangements and are always-treated rather than first-wave treated
  - The four direct-administered municipalities (Beijing, Tianjin, Shanghai, Chongqing)
  - 'Earlier directly-administered special cases: Jiyuan (Henan), Tianmen, Qianjiang and Xiantao (Hubei), Shihezi (Xinjiang)'
  - City districts, which remain under prefecture-city fiscal management
  - 'Enumerated provincial exceptions: Anhui city-district counties and the counties of Ma''anshan, Tongling, Huainan and Huaibei; Hubei''s Enshi autonomous prefecture'
  compliance: >
    The reform is an administrative reassignment of fiscal reporting and revenue
    channels, so compliance is near-mechanical once a county is listed; the open
    question is not evasion but the accuracy of the county-year list itself, which
    differs across secondary compilations.
  exposure_construction: >
    Build a county-year treatment indicator from the provincial reform documents
    (province, county list, effective budget year), harmonize county boundaries to
    a fixed administrative division year, drop city districts and always-treated
    jurisdictions, and merge with county fiscal and socioeconomic outcomes.
  required_identifiers:
  - county identifier with consistent 2000-2009 boundaries
  - prefecture identifier for spillover and peer-group construction
  - province identifier and provincial reform effective year
  - year
  spillovers: >
    In phased provinces the prefecture loses the fiscal channel over reformed
    counties and may redirect extraction or support toward its remaining counties,
    contaminating within-province controls; this is a documented design concern
    that led the companion study to drop those counties. In province-wide
    provinces the prefecture's role changes for all counties at once, muting this
    margin but not prefecture-level economic responses.
research_compatibility:
  outcome_domains:
  - 'county fiscal behavior: revenue effort, expenditure level and structure, productive versus consumptive spending'
  - county economic growth and per-capita GDP
  - intergovernmental information flow, monitoring, and yardstick competition among counties
  - county public-service provision (education, health) through the expenditure-structure channel
  affected_populations:
  - County governments and their fiscal departments in reform provinces
  - Residents and firms of reformed counties exposed to changed county expenditure incentives
  - Prefecture governments whose fiscal intermediation role is removed
  mechanism_channels:
  - 'information transparency: the province observes county fiscal accounts directly once the prefecture filter is removed, strengthening monitoring (the JRS paper interpretation)'
  - 'transfer bypass: provincial transfers and special grants reach the county without prefecture retention'
  - 'fixed revenue sharing: the county retention shares are fixed against the province, changing revenue-effort incentives'
  - 'weakened sub-provincial coordination and intensified horizontal fiscal competition among a larger set of peer counties (the companion Management World paper interpretation)'
  best_for:
  - Studies of how sub-provincial fiscal hierarchy changes county fiscal incentives, expenditure structure, and growth, using a staggered county-level adoption with documented provincial selection rules
  - Designs that can address selection into pilot counties (matching, fixed effects, or documented one-step province-wide adoption)
  - Questions about monitoring and information in multi-tier government, where the prefecture information filter is the object of interest
  not_good_for:
  - 'Claims that the reform is an intrinsically exogenous shock: provincial documents show deliberate selection of strong or poor counties'
  - Post-2009 questions about the centrally mandated rollout, which is a different implementation regime
  - Designs that cannot separate this reform from the concurrent rural tax-fee reform expansion and 2004-2006 agricultural tax abolition
  - Studies of the administrative empowerment (qiangxian kuquan) reforms, a related but distinct instrument that devolves administrative approval powers rather than re-routing the fiscal chain
design:
  claim_type: causal
  affordances:
  - Staggered province-level adoption in 2004 and 2005 with a clean pre-period back to 2000
  - Within-province contrasts between pilot and non-pilot counties in phased provinces, with documented selection criteria
  - One-step province-wide adoption in Anhui and Hubei that avoids within-province pilot selection
  - A verified exclusion set (always-treated jurisdictions, city districts) that clarifies the comparison pool
  candidate_designs:
  - Staggered difference-in-differences at the county-year level with county and year fixed effects, treating counties in 2004/2005 reform provinces after adoption
  - PSM-DID matching reformed counties to never-reformed counties in non-reform provinces on pre-2003 county characteristics, as in the author team's verified companion design
  - Anhui/Hubei-only province-wide-adoption DID to sidestep within-province pilot selection
  - Heterogeneity by the number of same-province peer counties and by pre-reform growth rank among peers, as reported in the JRS application
  identifying_variation: >
    Cross-county and over-time differences in exposure created by provincial
    adoption decisions in 2004-2005, conditional on county and year fixed
    effects. The identifying content rests on the assumption that, conditional on
    observables (or on matching), non-adopting provinces and their counties
    provide valid counterfactual trends; it does not come from random assignment.
  primary_strategy: >
    The JRS paper (Jia, Ning, and Zhang 2023) uses a county panel of 590 counties,
    2000-2009, and reports that counties in reform provinces adopt more proactive
    fiscal policy after the 2004/2005 reform, with stronger responses where a
    county has fewer competing peers or an extreme growth rank among peers, and
    with productive-spending increases followed by higher later growth. The
    verified companion program (Jia and Ning 2015; Ning and Zhao 2019) implements
    PSM-DID on national county panels with county-level treatment coding from
    provincial documents.
  estimand: >
    The average effect of entering the fiscal PMC regime on treated counties'
    fiscal behavior and expenditure structure (ATT in the PSM-DID applications),
    interpreted by the JRS paper as a monitoring-and-incentives response to
    improved province-county information transparency.
  treatment_variable: >
    County-year indicator for operating under the direct province-county fiscal
    linkage; the JRS paper reportedly codes it from province-level 2004/2005
    adoption, while the author team's verified convention codes county-by-county
    adoption years from provincial documents.
  comparison_logic: >
    Compare reformed counties with never-reformed counties in non-adopting
    provinces; exclude always-treated jurisdictions, city districts, and (in the
    verified companion design) the non-reformed counties of phased provinces
    because they are exposed to prefecture spillovers.
  estimation_notes: >
    The JRS paper's exact specification, reform-province list, peer-count
    construction, and county screens are known only from its abstract and are
    unverified. The verified companion design uses propensity scores from a Probit
    on pre-2003 county characteristics (within-province real per-capita GDP rank,
    sector shares, population density, urbanization, fiscal gap), kernel and
    k-nearest-neighbor matching with balance tests, and a 2000-2002 versus 2007
    DID contrast; it drops 2003 to limit anticipation and winsorizes at the 1st
    and 99th percentiles.
  assumptions:
  - Conditional on county fixed effects (and matching on pre-reform characteristics in the PSM-DID variant), non-adopting provinces' counties trace the counterfactual trend of treated counties
  - Provincial selection of pilot counties is captured by the observed selection criteria documented in provincial documents (economic strength or poverty status)
  - Concurrent reforms (rural tax-fee reform expansion, 2004-2006 agricultural tax abolition, township fiscal management reform) are either controlled or do not differentially target the same counties
  - Prefecture spillovers onto non-reformed counties in phased provinces are removed by excluding those counties from the comparison
  - The reform's effect on county behavior operates through the fiscal-linkage change rather than through simultaneous administrative empowerment pilots
  diagnostics:
  - Pre-trend and anticipation checks over 2000-2003 pre-reform years
  - Matching balance tests and common-support inspection in the PSM-DID design
  - Placebo or falsification using always-treated jurisdictions and city districts as out-of-scope groups
  - Rebuild the county-year coding from named provincial documents and test sensitivity to the conflicting secondary province-year lists
  - Interact treatment with the number of same-province peers and pre-reform growth rank to probe the monitoring/competition mechanism, as the JRS paper reports
threats:
- type: non_random_county_selection
  basis: documented
  condition: Provincial documents show pilot counties were chosen by economic characteristics — Henan's 2004 pilots favored strong counties, Jiangxi and Shanxi piloted in national poverty counties, Shaanxi mixed distressed and better-off counties — so adoption is selected on levels that also drive fiscal outcomes and growth.
  evidence_refs:
  - E4
  possible_diagnostics:
  - PSM-DID or other selection-on-observables corrections, as in the verified companion design
  - Restrict to Anhui and Hubei province-wide adoptions where within-province selection is absent
  - Test whether pre-reform characteristics predict adoption and whether matched balance holds
- type: concurrent_fiscal_reforms
  basis: documented
  condition: The treatment window overlaps the rural tax-fee reform expansion (2000-2003 staggered rollout) and the 2004-2006 agricultural tax abolition, as well as township-level fiscal management reforms, all of which change county fiscal constraints and could correlate with PMC adoption timing.
  evidence_refs:
  - E4
  - E6
  possible_diagnostics:
  - Control for agricultural tax reliance and rural tax-fee reform timing, as done in the related literature
  - Use the existing china-rural-tax-fee-reform-expansion record to construct the concurrent-reform exposure explicitly
  - Check robustness in provinces with low agricultural-tax dependence
- type: prefecture_spillover_contamination
  basis: documented
  condition: In phased provinces, removing some counties from the prefecture's fiscal channel changes the prefecture's behavior toward its remaining counties, so within-province non-pilot counties are not clean controls.
  evidence_refs:
  - E4
  possible_diagnostics:
  - Exclude non-reformed counties of phased provinces from the comparison pool, as the verified companion design does
  - Estimate prefecture-level responses directly to detect re-extraction or support redirection
- type: treatment_coding_disagreement
  basis: documented
  condition: Secondary compilations disagree on several province-year cells (for example Fujian 2003 versus 2004, Henan 2004 pilot versus 2005, Jilin 2005 versus 2006), and the JRS paper's own list is behind a paywall; a coding copied from a secondary list can misassign treatment years.

- type: bundled_treatment_mechanism

  condition: The reform bundles transfer bypass, fixed revenue sharing, and direct reporting; the JRS paper's information-transparency interpretation is one channel within the bundle, and the verified companion study instead emphasizes weakened sub-provincial coordination and intensified horizontal competition, so mechanism claims must be stated as interpretations, not established facts.
  evidence_refs:
  - E1

  - Separate outcome families that load differently on each channel (transfers received versus revenue effort versus expenditure structure)
  - Compare provinces whose reform packages differed in content

## Institutional Background

China's sub-provincial fiscal system long followed a "province manages city,
city manages county" (省管市、市管县) chain: prefecture city governments sat
between provinces and counties in revenue sharing, transfer allocation, budget
approval, and year-end settlement. After the 1994 tax-sharing reform recentralized
revenue upward, counties complained that prefectures lowered county retention
shares, retained transfers destined for counties, and pushed spending
responsibilities downward — the "city scraping the county" (市刮县) problem that
left many counties in fiscal distress [E4].

The fiscal province-managing-county (省直管县财政体制) reform attacked this by
removing the prefecture from the fiscal chain while leaving the administrative
hierarchy intact. Anhui's provincial government decided on 2004-01-21 to implement
the system province-wide from 2004, routing revenue and expenditure division,
transfer payments, fiscal settlement, fund scheduling, special-fund grants, debt
repayment, and work contacts directly to counties [E2]. Hubei implemented the same
regime province-wide from 2004 under 鄂政发〔2004〕20号, as its 2007 follow-up
opinion records [E3]. Henan piloted in selected strong counties in 2004; Hebei and
Jiangxi began pilots in 2005 （赣府发〔2005〕2号）; Shanxi （晋政发〔2006〕45
号） and Shaanxi （陕政发〔2006〕65号） followed in 2006; Jiangsu adopted
province-wide from 2007 under a standardized template that abolished city-county
budget and settlement relations and routed special grants directly to counties
[E4; E5]. This province-initiated phase ended when the Ministry of Finance's June
2009 opinion (财预〔2009〕78号） set a national end-2012 rollout target — a
different, centrally directed regime outside this record's boundary [E4, reported].

## What Changed

For a reformed county, the fiscal counterpart changes from the prefecture to the
province: revenue-sharing ratios are fixed against the province, provincial
transfers and special grants reach the county without prefecture retention, budgets
and final accounts are reported directly to the provincial finance department, and
fund scheduling and settlement bypass the prefecture [E2; E3; E5]. The JRS paper
interprets this as an information-transparency change — the province now observes
county fiscal accounts without the prefecture filter, which strengthens monitoring
and counties' incentives in growth competition [E1]. The companion Management
World study instead emphasizes weakened sub-provincial coordination and
intensified horizontal fiscal competition among a larger peer set [E6]. Both are
interpretations of the same bundled institutional change, not separately verified
channels [analytical inference].

## Implementation and Assignment

Assignment is a provincial government decision, staggered by province and, in
phased provinces, selective by county. Anhui and Hubei reformed nearly all
counties in one step in 2004, with enumerated exceptions (Anhui's city districts
and the counties of Ma'anshan, Tongling, Huainan, and Huaibei; Hubei's Enshi
autonomous prefecture) [E2; E3]. Phased provinces chose pilot counties on
documented economic criteria: Henan's 2004 pilots favored economically strong
counties, Jiangxi and Shanxi piloted in national poverty-designated counties, and
Shaanxi combined fiscally distressed counties with two better-off ones [E4]. This
selection is a first-order design feature: adoption is not random and not a
threshold rule, so the abstract's "exogenous reform" label [E1] must not be read
as a universal assignment property.

The verified author-team coding convention excludes always-treated jurisdictions
(Zhejiang, Hainan, the four municipalities, and special cases such as Jiyuan,
Tianmen, Qianjiang, Xiantao, and Shihezi), drops city districts and
boundary-changed counties, and removes non-reformed counties in the six phased
provinces from the control pool because prefectures can redirect extraction or
support toward them [E4]. Whether the JRS paper codes treatment province-by-year
or county-by-county within phased provinces is not visible in its abstract [E1].

## Why This Creates Empirical Variation

The 2004-2005 first wave creates staggered county exposure with a clean pre-period
back to 2000, two distinct adoption modes (one-step province-wide versus phased
pilot selection), and a documented selection mechanism that can be modeled rather
than assumed away. County fiscal outcomes are available annually in the MOF county
fiscal statistics compendia and the county statistical yearbook [E4]. The design
affords staggered DID at the county-year level, PSM-DID against never-reformed
provinces' counties, and an Anhui/Hubei-only specification that sidesteps
within-province pilot selection entirely [E1; E4; E6].

## Identification Risks

The main risks are documented rather than hypothetical: pilot selection on county
economic characteristics [E4]; overlap with the rural tax-fee reform expansion and
the 2004-2006 agricultural tax abolition (see the related record
china-rural-tax-fee-reform-expansion); prefecture spillovers onto non-reformed
counties in phased provinces [E4]; conflicting secondary province-year coding
lists that can silently shift treatment years [E1; E4]; and the bundled nature of
the treatment, which mixes transfer bypass, revenue-share fixing, and reporting
changes so that no single mechanism is established [E1; E4; E6; analytical
inference]. The JRS application's own coding remains unverified behind the
publisher paywall [E1].

## Data Requirements

A reuse needs an annual county panel (2000-2009 for the JRS design), county fiscal
revenue and expenditure detail with the own-revenue/transfer decomposition, county
GDP and population, prefecture and province identifiers with consistent
boundaries, a county-year reform indicator rebuilt from the named provincial
documents, and concurrent-reform exposures (rural tax-fee reform timing,
agricultural-tax reliance) [E2; E3; E4]. The county fiscal compendia are
restricted-circulation MOF publications; dataset acquisition paths belong in the
companion data repository `Econ Data Know-How`, not in this record.
