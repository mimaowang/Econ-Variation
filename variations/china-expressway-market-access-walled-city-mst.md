## Institutional Background

China's National Expressway Network was planned as a national backbone connecting major cities and regional corridors. The Ministry of Transport record identifies the 2005 plan as Jiao Guihua Fa [2005] No. 40, while the NDRC summary states that the plan was approved by the State Council in 2004 and organized around the 7918 network. The plan set multi-year construction milestones rather than a single treatment date. [E1; E3]

## What Changed

During the 2000–2009 period studied by Jing and Liao, rapid expressway expansion changed travel times and market access across Chinese counties. The empirical object is not simply whether a county was named in a national plan. It is the continuous county-year market-access change created by a dynamically expanding network, with prominent and peripheral locations retained in the analysis. [E2]

## Implementation and Assignment

The paper constructs a dynamic hypothetical minimum-spanning-tree network. Historical walled-city characteristics predict the target cities that the simulated network ultimately connects; the network's expansion path then produces predicted market access by year. This predicted measure instruments observed market access. It is therefore a constructed assignment mechanism, not a claim that nineteenth-century walls directly assigned modern manufacturing outcomes. [E2]

## Why This Creates Empirical Variation

Counties differ in how their observed market access changes as the expressway network expands, and the walled-city MST construction supplies variation intended to be unrelated to contemporary local shocks. The resulting panel IV design can study rapid infrastructure expansion while keeping economically important locations in the sample. Because better access can attract firms and factors from other counties, the same variation also creates a spatial-equilibrium question: a local loss may be part of a network-wide reallocation rather than a failure of the infrastructure. [E2; analytical inference]

## Identification Risks

The core risks are exclusion through persistent historical urban advantages, weak or unstable first stages, sensitivity to simulated network costs and target-city prediction, and spatial relocation across counties. The official plan supports the existence and structure of the national network, but it does not establish that every county-year exposure follows the paper's constructed instrument. The paper's diagnostics and the required reconstruction of the network are therefore part of the design, not optional appendices. [E1; E2; analytical inference]

## Data Requirements

A usable application needs county or firm outcomes with stable county and year identifiers, observed market access or travel-time measures, the dynamic walled-city MST predicted measure, county GIS geometry, and harmonized expressway opening data. If the outcome is firm-level, firms must be linked to counties and the analyst must decide whether the estimand concerns local production, firm location, or network-wide reallocation. [E2]

## Evidence Notes

E1 and E3 establish the national expressway planning regime, legal metadata, 7918 structure, and construction horizon; they do not establish the paper's county-year treatment coding. E2 establishes the paper's constructed instrument, empirical period, outcomes, and stated validity exercises; it does not substitute for independent replication of the GIS network, historical inputs, or county crosswalks. Those reconstruction limits remain explicit blockers rather than being filled with assumptions.
